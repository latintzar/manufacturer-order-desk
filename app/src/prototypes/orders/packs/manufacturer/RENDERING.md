# Product image construction

Peter selected one angled image per visual family on 11 October 2026. The presale checkpoint stays bounded; further image production belongs to onboarding.

## Primary-source findings

Product-visualization practitioner Will Gibbons ties believable images to real-world model dimensions, correctly sized edge transitions and textures, physically plausible materials, and a camera whose distance, focal length and focus work together. Surface detail should respect the manufacturing process. His general preference for longer product lenses is a starting point, not a requirement for our long-stock framing. [Realistic rendering](https://www.willgibbons.com/articles/realistic-rendering).

KeyShot documents precise camera targeting and perspective controls. Record their numerical settings with the model instead of relying on a style adjective. [Perspective](https://manuals.keyshot.com/keyshot2024/manual/perspective.html). Its area-light controls separate emitted light, colour temperature and reflected source visibility, making a reproducible lighting rig possible. [Area lights](https://manual.keyshot.com/manual/materials/material-types/light-sources/area-light/).

FutureDeluxe lists its Apple MacBook Air project. That establishes a relevant studio reference, not a published recipe for its lenses or shaders. [Apple project](https://futuredeluxe.com/work/apple). This review found no public basis for claiming access to Apple's internal rendering settings.

## Metal-M implementation decisions

These are our application of the sources and Peter's direction, rather than claims about Apple's process.

1. Select a real representative catalogue section and purchasable stock length. Record the SKU, dimensions, units and selection reason. A sample photograph establishes finish; the catalogue establishes size. Sales popularity remains unknown until the company supplies it.
2. Use one part at physical scale. For long stock, show a complete near end at an angle and let the shaft continue outside the frame. The near section remains legible while perspective makes the receding shaft slimmer. Keep straight edges straight. Compact ornaments retain their evidenced complete form.
3. Record camera position, target, sensor, focal length, focus distance and aperture. The accepted 064 geometry uses a 55 mm camera; preserve its actual framing as the reference. Validate projected near-end bounds before rendering.
4. Record exterior, interior, raw cut face and coating separately. A saw-cut face may have a different grain and reflectance from an oxidised exterior. A bevel, rolled radius and sharp cut edge describe different geometry. Preserve only source-supported features.
5. Use the same neutral-grey seamless studio, directional key, exposure, white balance and shadow character. A subtle backdrop roughness may provide natural variation, but must remain subordinate to the metal and shared across the series. Compare the whole selection at the same display scale.
6. Keep texture scale tied to the part. Compression blocks are not pits; a scratch is not a groove. Hammering that changes a silhouette requires structural relief, while fine surface variation belongs in the material.
7. Spend after structure and source bindings pass. Reuse judged assets where they match the final frame. Record rejects and cost; stop at the checkpoint.

## Run-1 result

The 064 angled image replaced a short sample and inconsistent multi-view attempts. Its master is 25 × 5 × 3,000 mm, with the long shaft leaving the frame. The multi-view outputs and rejected attempts remain in private audit storage. The broader 26-call repair batch was cancelled before any call launched.
